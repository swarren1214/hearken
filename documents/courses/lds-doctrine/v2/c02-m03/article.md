# Faith, repentance and baptism

## The doctrine of Christ

The Book of Mormon uses a specific phrase for the way a person comes to Christ: "the doctrine of Christ." Nephi uses it in 2 Nephi 31 and 32, and the risen Christ uses it in 3 Nephi 11, where he says, "this is my doctrine" and describes repentance, belief and baptism (3 Nephi 11:31–39). In these chapters the phrase does not mean all of Christian teaching. It names a particular pattern: faith in Christ, repentance, baptism, the gift of the Holy Ghost, and enduring to the end.

Article of Faith 4, which you read in module 1.1, gives the first part of that pattern in compact form: "the first principles and ordinances of the Gospel are: first, Faith in the Lord Jesus Christ; second, Repentance; third, Baptism by immersion for the remission of sins; fourth, Laying on of hands for the gift of the Holy Ghost."

This module studies the first steps, faith, repentance and baptism, mainly through 2 Nephi 31. The next module takes up the Holy Ghost and enduring. Together they cover the pattern Latter-day Saints call the doctrine of Christ.

## Christ's own baptism

Nephi begins with a question. If Jesus, "being holy," needed to be baptized "to fulfil all righteousness," how much more do "we, being unholy" (2 Nephi 31:5)? The phrase "fulfil all righteousness" comes from Matthew 3:15, where Jesus answers John's hesitation to baptize him.

Nephi then explains what Christ's baptism showed. Jesus "humbleth himself before the Father, and witnesseth unto the Father that he would be obedient unto him in keeping his commandments" (verse 7). After he was baptized, "the Holy Ghost descended upon him in the form of a dove" (verse 8). And it showed "the straitness of the path, and the narrowness of the gate" (verse 9). Christ, in this reading, was baptized not because he needed forgiveness but as an act of obedience and an example to follow.

Verse 10 draws the conclusion in Christ's own words: "Follow thou me." Nephi adds, "can we follow Jesus save we shall be willing to keep the commandments of the Father?"

## Faith and repentance

In this pattern, faith in Jesus Christ comes first, and it is not passive. Nephi describes it as following the Son "with full purpose of heart, acting no hypocrisy and no deception before God, but with real intent" (verse 13). Later he says that people enter the path "by the word of Christ with unshaken faith in him, relying wholly upon the merits of him who is mighty to save" (verse 19).

Repentance is joined to faith throughout the chapter. The Father's voice says, "Repent ye, repent ye, and be baptized in the name of my Beloved Son" (verse 11). In 3 Nephi 11:32, Christ says the Father "commandeth all men, everywhere, to repent and believe in me."

These passages present repentance as turning toward God, made possible by Christ, and expressed in action. Alma 36, which you will read in the next module, gives a vivid narrative of one person's repentance. For now, note how the doctrinal passages connect it to faith and baptism rather than treating it as a separate topic.

## Baptism: the gate

Nephi's image for baptism is a gate. "The gate by which ye should enter is repentance and baptism by water; and then cometh a remission of your sins by fire and by the Holy Ghost" (verse 17). After entering, a person is "in this strait and narrow path which leads to eternal life" (verse 18).

The image does two things. It marks baptism as a beginning, not an end; a gate opens onto a path. And it places baptism and the Holy Ghost together: water baptism is followed by "the baptism of fire and of the Holy Ghost" (verse 13). In Latter-day Saint practice, the gift of the Holy Ghost is conferred by the laying on of hands after baptism, as Article of Faith 4 states.

The New Testament gives a parallel pattern. On the day of Pentecost, Peter told his hearers, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost" (Acts 2:38). Latter-day Saints cite this passage alongside 2 Nephi 31 as evidence that the pattern is the same in both books.

## How baptism is performed

Two Book of Mormon passages describe the manner of baptism. In 3 Nephi 11:23–26, Christ gives the words to be spoken, "Having authority given me of Jesus Christ, I baptize you in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. Amen," and then says, "shall ye immerse them in the water, and come forth again out of the water." Article of Faith 4 specifies "baptism by immersion."

The same passage ties baptism to authority. Christ gives Nephi and others "power" to baptize (verses 21–22), and the prescribed words begin, "Having authority given me of Jesus Christ." Article of Faith 5 states the general principle: a person must be "called of God, by prophecy, and by the laying on of hands by those who are in authority," to administer the ordinances of the gospel. In Latter-day Saint teaching, authority is part of what makes a baptism valid. That is one reason the Church baptizes converts even if they were baptized in another tradition.

## The baptismal covenant

Latter-day Saint teaching describes baptism as entering a covenant, a two-way promise with God. The clearest scriptural description is in Mosiah 18, where Alma invites people at the waters of Mormon to be baptized.

Alma describes those who are ready as "desirous to come into the fold of God, and to be called his people," and "willing to bear one another's burdens, that they may be light; yea, and are willing to mourn with those that mourn; yea, and comfort those that stand in need of comfort, and to stand as witnesses of God at all times and in all things, and in all places" (verses 8–9). Baptism, he says, is "a witness before him that ye have entered into a covenant with him, that ye will serve him and keep his commandments, that he may pour out his Spirit more abundantly upon you" (verse 10).

Notice the structure. The people promise to serve God, keep his commandments and care for one another. God's promise is to pour out his Spirit. When you describe the baptismal covenant, cite Mosiah 18:8–10 for these specific terms, and 2 Nephi 31:13 for the promise to take upon oneself the name of Christ.

## A sequence, not a checklist

It is easy to turn the doctrine of Christ into a checklist: faith, done; repentance, done; baptism, done. Nephi resists that reading directly. After the gate, he asks, "I would ask if all is done? Behold, I say unto you, Nay" (verse 19). The next module takes up what follows.

For now, notice how Nephi ends the chapter: "this is the way; and there is none other way nor name given under heaven whereby man can be saved in the kingdom of God. And now, behold, this is the doctrine of Christ, and the only and true doctrine of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, which is one God, without end" (verse 21). The pattern is presented as a way of life centered on Christ, not a set of tasks.

## Describing it well

A careful short explanation might read: "Latter-day Saints call the pattern in 2 Nephi 31 'the doctrine of Christ': faith in Jesus Christ, repentance, baptism by immersion, and the gift of the Holy Ghost, followed by enduring to the end. Nephi calls repentance and baptism 'the gate' to a path leading to eternal life (verses 17–18). In Mosiah 18:8–10, baptism is a covenant to serve God, keep his commandments and care for others, with God's promise of his Spirit. Article of Faith 5 adds that the ordinance requires authority from God."

That explanation names its passages, uses their own terms, and describes the practice without claiming more than the sources state.

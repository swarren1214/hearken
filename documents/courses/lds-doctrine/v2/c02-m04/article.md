# The Holy Ghost and enduring to the end

## After the gate

At the end of 2 Nephi 31, after describing faith, repentance and baptism, Nephi asks whether "all is done" and answers, "Nay" (verse 19). This module takes up what comes next in the doctrine of Christ: the gift of the Holy Ghost, the guidance it is said to give, and enduring to the end.

These subjects involve claims about spiritual experience: that the Holy Ghost teaches, witnesses and guides. As throughout this course, your task is to describe those claims accurately from their sources. You will not be asked about your own experience, and nothing in this module assesses belief. A careful description of what Latter-day Saint scripture says about the Holy Ghost is a skill like any other.

## Who the Holy Ghost is

You met the Holy Ghost's nature in module 1.3. Doctrine and Covenants 130:22 says the Holy Ghost "has not a body of flesh and bones, but is a personage of Spirit," and adds, "Were it not so, the Holy Ghost could not dwell in us." The Church's Godhead page describes the Holy Ghost as the messenger and revealer of the Father and the Son.

Several passages describe the Holy Ghost's work as witness. In 3 Nephi 11:32, Christ says that "the Holy Ghost beareth record of the Father and me." In 2 Nephi 31:18, the Holy Ghost "witnesses of the Father and the Son." Jesus tells his disciples in John 14:26 that "the Comforter, which is the Holy Ghost, whom the Father will send in my name, he shall teach you all things, and bring all things to your remembrance, whatsoever I have said unto you."

Keep these roles distinct when you write about them: witnessing of the Father and Son, teaching, and bringing to remembrance. Each passage supports a particular description.

## The gift of the Holy Ghost

Latter-day Saint teaching distinguishes the influence of the Holy Ghost, which anyone may feel, from the gift of the Holy Ghost, given after baptism by the laying on of hands (Article of Faith 4). The scriptures you have read reflect this sequence. In 2 Nephi 31:12, the voice of the Son says, "He that is baptized in my name, to him will the Father give the Holy Ghost, like unto me." Acts 2:38 promises "the gift of the Holy Ghost" to those who repent and are baptized.

Doctrine and Covenants 130:23 adds a caution: "A man may receive the Holy Ghost, and it may descend upon him and not tarry with him." The gift, in this teaching, is a continuing relationship that can be lost, not a single event.

## "Feast upon the words of Christ"

In 2 Nephi 32, Nephi answers a question he senses his readers are asking: what should they do after entering the path? His first answer is about the words of Christ. "Angels speak by the power of the Holy Ghost; wherefore, they speak the words of Christ. Wherefore, I said unto you, feast upon the words of Christ; for behold, the words of Christ will tell you all things what ye should do" (verse 3).

His second answer is about the Holy Ghost: "if ye will enter in by the way, and receive the Holy Ghost, it will show unto you all things what ye should do" (verse 5). The two answers belong together. In Nephi's teaching, the words of Christ and the Holy Ghost both guide those on the path.

Then Nephi turns to prayer. "The evil spirit teacheth not a man to pray, but teacheth him that he must not pray" (verse 8). "Ye must pray always, and not faint; ... ye shall pray unto the Father in the name of Christ, that he will consecrate thy performance unto thee, that thy performance may be for the welfare of thy soul" (verse 9). Notice again the pattern of prayer from module 1.3: to the Father, in the name of Christ.

## Moroni's promise

Near the very end of the Book of Mormon, Moroni addresses future readers. He asks them to "remember how merciful the Lord hath been" and "ponder it in your hearts" (Moroni 10:3). Then he makes a promise: "ask God, the Eternal Father, in the name of Christ, if these things are not true; and if ye shall ask with a sincere heart, with real intent, having faith in Christ, he will manifest the truth of it unto you, by the power of the Holy Ghost" (verse 4). And: "by the power of the Holy Ghost ye may know the truth of all things" (verse 5).

Read the promise for its conditions. It asks for remembering, pondering and asking; it specifies asking the Father in Christ's name; and it names sincerity, real intent and faith in Christ. Latter-day Saint teaching often refers to this passage simply as "Moroni's promise." When you describe it, quote its conditions as well as its promise. A summary that leaves the conditions out misdescribes the passage.

Moroni 7 gives a related principle for discernment that Latter-day Saints often cite: "every thing which inviteth to do good, and to persuade to believe in Christ, is sent forth by the power and gift of Christ" (Moroni 7:16). That passage is about the Spirit of Christ given to every person, and you can note it as a related source rather than a description of the gift of the Holy Ghost.

## Repentance and joy: Alma 36

Alma 36 is a father's account of his own conversion. Alma the Younger tells his son Helaman that he once fought against the Church, and describes three days and nights of torment as he remembered his sins (verses 12–16). Then he remembered his father's teaching "concerning the coming of one Jesus Christ, a Son of God, to atone for the sins of the world" (verse 17), and cried out in his heart, "O Jesus, thou Son of God, have mercy on me" (verse 18).

What follows is one of the most vivid passages in the Book of Mormon: "I could remember my pains no more" (verse 19); "what joy, and what marvelous light I did behold" (verse 20); "there can be nothing so exquisite and sweet as was my joy" (verse 21). From then on, Alma says, he "labored without ceasing" to bring others to "taste of the exceeding joy" he had tasted, that they "might also be born of God, and be filled with the Holy Ghost" (verse 24).

Alma 36 is a narrative of personal experience, and it should be described as one: Alma's account, told to his son, of his own repentance. It is also a text Latter-day Saints use to teach what repentance through Christ can mean. Both statements are accurate; keep them distinct.

## Enduring to the end

The final step of the doctrine of Christ is to endure. In 2 Nephi 31:15 the Father's voice says, "He that endureth to the end, the same shall be saved," and Nephi concludes that "unless a man shall endure to the end, in following the example of the Son of the living God, he cannot be saved" (verse 16).

Nephi then describes what enduring looks like, and it is not grim persistence. "Ye must press forward with a steadfastness in Christ, having a perfect brightness of hope, and a love of God and of all men. Wherefore, if ye shall press forward, feasting upon the word of Christ, and endure to the end, behold, thus saith the Father: Ye shall have eternal life" (verse 20).

Read closely, enduring in this verse has content: steadfastness in Christ, hope, love of God and others, and feasting on the word. It is the same pattern as the rest of the doctrine of Christ, continued through life.

## Describing it well

A careful short explanation might read: "In the Latter-day Saint pattern Nephi calls the doctrine of Christ, baptism is followed by the gift of the Holy Ghost (2 Nephi 31:12–13; Article of Faith 4), which Nephi says 'will show unto you all things what ye should do' (2 Nephi 32:5). Nephi teaches that those on the path must 'feast upon the words of Christ,' pray to the Father in Christ's name, and 'endure to the end' by pressing forward with hope and love (2 Nephi 31:20). Moroni 10:4–5 promises that those who ask God sincerely, with real intent and faith in Christ, can know truth by the power of the Holy Ghost."

The explanation describes what the texts promise and teach. It does not tell the reader what to believe or claim anything about any particular person's experience.

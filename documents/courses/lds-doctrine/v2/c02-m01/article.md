# The plan of salvation

## A plan with many names

Latter-day Saints often describe the whole of God's dealings with his children as a plan. The Book of Mormon gives it several names, sometimes in a single chapter. In Alma 42, a father teaching his son calls it "the great plan of salvation" (verse 5), "the great plan of happiness" (verse 8), "the plan of redemption" (verse 11) and "the plan of mercy" (verse 15). Each name highlights something different: rescue, joy, buying back what was lost, and mercy toward those who could not save themselves.

The Church's Gospel Topics page on the plan of salvation describes it as the plan that contains the laws and ordinances needed for eternal life. This module traces the plan's main parts through the canonical texts you will read and the Church's own summary. Later courses go deeper into each part. Here the goal is the overall shape, and the habit of keeping track of which source says what.

## God's stated purpose: Moses 1:39

The Book of Moses, in the Pearl of Great Price, records a vision in which Moses asks God why the creations exist and how they were made (Moses 1:30). The answer begins with a refusal to say everything: "For mine own purpose have I made these things. Here is wisdom and it remaineth in me" (verse 31). God then tells Moses that he created by his Only Begotten Son (verses 32–33), that there are "worlds without number" (verse 33), and that "only an account of this earth, and the inhabitants thereof" would be given (verse 35).

The vision ends with one of the most quoted verses in Latter-day Saint scripture: "For behold, this is my work and my glory—to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man" (verse 39).

Read the passage as a whole and two things stand out. First, it names a purpose: immortality and eternal life for human beings. Second, it is openly selective. Moses is told that God has created far more than he will learn about. A careful student keeps both points. Moses 1:39 is a strong source for the claim that, in Latter-day Saint scripture, God's work centers on human salvation. It is not a source for detailed claims about other worlds, which the passage deliberately declines to give.

## Before this life: Abraham 3

Latter-day Saint teaching holds that human beings lived before mortal birth. The Gospel Topics page puts it this way: before birth, people lived with heavenly parents as their spirit children. The canonical passage most often cited for the premortal council is Abraham 3:22–28.

In that passage, Abraham is shown "the intelligences that were organized before the world was," among them "many of the noble and great ones" (verse 22). God tells Abraham, "thou wast chosen before thou wast born" (verse 23). Then "one among them that was like unto God" proposes making an earth where these spirits may dwell, adding, "we will prove them herewith, to see if they will do all things whatsoever the Lord their God shall command them" (verses 24–25).

Verse 26 introduces two terms worth learning. Those "who keep their first estate shall be added upon," and those who "keep their second estate shall have glory added upon their heads for ever and ever." Latter-day Saint teaching generally reads the first estate as premortal life and the second as mortal life. The verse itself uses the terms without defining them, so when you explain them, attribute the explanation.

The passage ends with a choice. The Lord asks, "Whom shall I send?" One "like unto the Son of Man" answers, "Here am I, send me," and another answers the same. The Lord sends the first; the second "was angry, and kept not his first estate; and, at that day, many followed after him" (verses 27–28).

## The council and agency: Moses 4

Moses 4:1–4 tells the same council from another angle, and it adds the reason for the rejection. Satan proposed to "redeem all mankind, that one soul shall not be lost," and asked for God's honor (verse 1). The Beloved Son answered, "Father, thy will be done, and the glory be thine forever" (verse 2). Satan, the passage says, "rebelled against me, and sought to destroy the agency of man" (verse 3), and was cast down.

Put the two passages side by side and notice what each contributes. Abraham 3 gives the setting and the purpose, proving the spirits, and the two volunteers. Moses 4 names the issue: agency. Neither passage says everything the other says. When you write about the council, cite the passage that supports each detail. The Gospel Topics page adds a detail of its own, that a third of Heavenly Father's spirit children followed Lucifer, and cites Revelation 12 for it. That detail comes from the page and the passage it cites, not from Abraham 3, which says only that "many followed after him."

Agency will be the subject of the next module. For now, notice how central it is: in Moses 4, the threat to the plan is not described as a threat to human happiness or to God's power, but to human choice.

## Mortal life: bodies, choices and trials

The Gospel Topics page lists several purposes of mortal life. Gaining a physical body is one of them. Agency is another: the page describes choice as essential to the plan, including the choice to repent and to receive ordinances such as baptism and confirmation. The page also says that earth life brings both joy and adversity, including temptation, opposition and trials, and that these can help people become more like their Heavenly Father.

Abraham 3:25 frames mortality as a test: "we will prove them herewith." Alma 42:4 calls it "a probationary time, a time to repent and serve God," and verse 10 "a preparatory state." These descriptions fit together. Mortality, in this teaching, is neither an accident nor a punishment. It is a stage with a purpose.

## Christ's Atonement at the center

Every name for the plan in Alma 42 points to the same center. "The plan of mercy could not be brought about except an atonement should be made," Alma says, and "therefore God himself atoneth for the sins of the world" (verse 15). The Gospel Topics page says that Jesus Christ overcame both physical and spiritual death, and that his Atonement was necessary because no unclean thing can dwell with God.

Alma 42 is also careful about how mercy works. Mercy does not cancel justice: "What, do ye suppose that mercy can rob justice? I say unto you, Nay; not one whit" (verse 25). Instead, "mercy claimeth the penitent, and mercy cometh because of the atonement" (verse 23). This is an important point for accurate description. In this teaching, the Atonement is how justice and mercy are both satisfied, not a suspension of justice.

## After this life

The Gospel Topics page summarizes what follows death. At death the spirit separates from the body and continues to live. Those who have lived righteously enter a state the page calls spirit paradise, citing Alma's description of "a state of rest, a state of peace." Because of Christ's Atonement, all people will be resurrected, their spirits and bodies reunited. A final judgment follows, in which, according to the page, people are judged according to their desires and their obedience to the commandments.

Alma 42:23 compresses much of this into one sentence: "the atonement bringeth to pass the resurrection of the dead; and the resurrection of the dead bringeth back men into the presence of God; and thus they are restored into his presence, to be judged according to their works."

Later courses cover the spirit world, resurrection and judgment in depth, including the degrees of glory described in Doctrine and Covenants 76. For this module, the outline is enough: premortal life, the council, mortal life, the Atonement, death, resurrection and judgment.

## Keeping the outline honest

The plan of salvation is often taught with a diagram: circles and arrows from premortal life to the kingdoms of glory. Diagrams are useful. They also make every part look equally certain and equally detailed, which the sources are not.

As you build your own outline, keep the source ledger habit. For each stage, write the passage or page that teaches it. Mark where the canon is explicit, where the Church's summary adds detail, and where an explanation is interpretation. "Abraham 3:26 says those who keep their second estate will have glory added upon their heads" is a quotation. "The second estate is mortal life" is a widely taught reading of it. Both belong in your notes, labeled differently.

Above all, notice what the sources themselves put at the center. Alma's names for the plan, Moses 1:39's statement of purpose, and the Church's summary all point to the same place: the Atonement of Jesus Christ and the eternal life of God's children.
